CE223 - Earthquake Protective Systems

This course explores earthquake protective systems with a focus on base isolation and energy dissipation at the system level. The projects below combine digitized experimental hysteresis loops, equivalent linear SDOF models, and both time-domain and frequency-domain solution techniques to quantify seismic performance.

Interactive dashboards

SDOF damping model comparisons

Compares Kelvin–Voigt, hysteretic, and fractional Kelvin–Voigt SDOF models. Includes harmonic hysteresis loops, storage/loss stiffness \(K_1(\omega), K_2(\omega)\), numerical energy dissipation per cycle, and frequency-domain earthquake response.

Base isolators and equivalent SDOF response

Uses digitized base isolator hysteresis curves to build an equivalent linear SDOF, iterate on effective stiffness and damping, and compare Newmark and FFT responses to the Kobe KBU090 ground motion. Also links to the underlying isolator hysteresis metrics.

Technical focus

Hysteresis and energy dissipation
Loop area \(\oint f\,du\) as a measure of mechanical energy dissipated per cycle, and its link to loss stiffness \(K_2(\omega)\).
Equivalent linear SDOF modeling
Mapping peak isolator displacement \(U_{\max}\) to shear strain, storage stiffness \(K_1\), and equivalent damping ratio \(\zeta_{\mathrm{eff}}\).
Time vs frequency domain
Comparing Newmark's method in the time domain with FFT-based transfer-function solutions \(U(\omega) = H(\omega)\,\ddot{U}_g(\omega)\) for the same SDOF parameters.
