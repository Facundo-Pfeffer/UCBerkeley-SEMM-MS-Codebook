CE223 - Earthquake Protective Systems

Objective

The goal is to characterize earthquake protective systems—base isolation and energy-dissipating mechanisms—and to predict their seismic response. Work is organized in two strands: (1) base isolator design, from digitized test curves to equivalent linear SDOF and time-history analysis; (2) SDOF damping model comparison, comparing viscous, hysteretic, and fractional damping under harmonic and earthquake loading. Each strand produces interactive dashboards that show what is computed at each step and why.

Base isolator strand

A rigid superstructure (mass \(m\)) is supported by 4 high-damping rubber (HDR) bearings. Test data give force–displacement hysteresis loops at several shear strain levels. The objective is to turn these into equivalent linear properties and then compute the response to a real ground motion (Kobe KBU090).

  1. Step (a): From scanned hysteresis plots, digitize the loops and compute per-bearing storage stiffness \(K_1\), loss stiffness \(K_2\), and effective damping ratio \(\zeta_{\mathrm{eff}}\) as functions of shear strain. This is the input for the next step.
  2. Step (b): Approximate the isolation system as a single-degree-of-freedom (SDOF) oscillator with system stiffness \(k = 4K_1\) and equivalent damping. Because \(K_1\) and \(\zeta_{\mathrm{eff}}\) depend on strain, an iterative procedure is used: assume a peak displacement, read off \(K_1\) and \(\zeta_{\mathrm{eff}}\), run Newmark’s method, update the peak from the response, and repeat until convergence.
  3. Step (c): Solve the same converged SDOF using the FFT (frequency-domain transfer function) and plot both solutions together for comparison.

Two dashboards support this strand: the first shows the hysteresis metrics and trends (Step a); the second shows the iteration, convergence, and Newmark vs FFT response (Steps b–c).

SDOF damping models strand

Three linear damping models—Kelvin–Voigt (viscous), hysteretic (structural), and fractional Kelvin–Voigt—are compared under prescribed harmonic displacement and under earthquake excitation. The objective is to see how storage stiffness \(K_1(\omega)\), loss stiffness \(K_2(\omega)\), and energy dissipated per cycle (EDC) depend on frequency, and how that affects earthquake response when using the correct dynamic stiffness \(\hat{R}(\omega)\) in the transfer function.

One full dashboard explains the models, shows hysteresis loops and EDC vs frequency, and includes the frequency-domain earthquake response. A second link gives a compact loops-only view.

Interactive dashboards

Base isolator hysteresis (Step a)

Digitized force–displacement loops at four shear strain levels. Per-bearing \(K_1\), \(K_2\), \(\zeta_{\mathrm{eff}}\), and energy per cycle; trend plots vs strain. Input for the equivalent-SDOF iteration.

Base isolator iteration and response (Steps b–c)

Equivalent-SDOF fixed-point iteration (4 bearings, system \(k = 4K_1\)). Convergence of shear strain and \(\zeta_{\mathrm{eff}}\); Newmark vs FFT response (displacement, velocity, absolute acceleration) to Kobe KBU090.

SDOF damping model comparisons

Models A (viscous), B (hysteretic), C (fractional). Harmonic hysteresis loops, \(K_1(\omega)\), \(K_2(\omega)\), EDC vs \(\omega\), and frequency-domain earthquake response for all three.

SDOF hysteresis loops (compact)

Force–displacement loops only for Models A, B, C at selected frequencies. Lightweight view without the full theory and EDC plots.

Technical focus

Hysteresis and energy dissipation
Loop area \(\oint f\,du\) as energy dissipated per cycle and its link to loss stiffness \(K_2(\omega)\).
Equivalent linear SDOF
Mapping peak displacement to shear strain, \(K_1\), and \(\zeta_{\mathrm{eff}}\); system stiffness \(k = 4K_1\) for four bearings in parallel.
Time vs frequency domain
Newmark’s method vs FFT transfer function \(U(\omega) = H(\omega)\,\ddot{U}_g(\omega)\) for the same SDOF.
